# Go Green Sod — what only the client can do

The website can be the best sod resource in Metro Atlanta and still lose to
Super-Sod, because the things that decide a local search mostly live outside the
website. This file is that half of the work.

`LAUNCH.md` covers what must be true before the domain goes live. This file
covers what makes the business findable and trusted once it is. Two items appear
in both because they block launch *and* marketing: the street address and the
Google Business Profile.

Ordered by leverage. Item 1 outranks everything else on this page combined.

---

## 1. Google Business Profile — start today, not at launch

For a local delivery business this is worth more than every on-page change in
this repo. It is free and takes about twenty minutes.

**Start now regardless of when the site goes live.** Verification can take one to
two weeks, and postcard verification cannot be rushed. It runs in parallel with
the build.

- [ ] **Claim the profile** at business.google.com.
- [ ] **Set it up as a service-area business, not a storefront.** Go Green Sod is
      delivery only with no pickup, so customers must not be sent to the farm.
      Google still needs the real address for verification; a service-area
      business simply does not display it publicly.
- [ ] **Primary category: "Sod supplier."** Not "Landscaper" and not
      "Landscaping supply store" — the primary category is the single strongest
      signal for which searches the profile appears in. Secondary categories can
      be added after.
- [ ] **Service areas:** name the towns and counties actually served. The site
      already lists them (Atlanta, Alpharetta, Marietta, Roswell, Sandy Springs,
      Duluth, Johns Creek, Kennesaw, Woodstock, Buford, Lawrenceville, Suwanee,
      plus Fulton, Gwinnett, Cobb, Cherokee, Forsyth and DeKalb counties).
      **These must match the site** — that list needs confirming either way.
- [ ] **Hours: Monday to Saturday, 8am to 6pm.** This is what the site publishes
      in its structured data. If the real hours differ, tell NEXTLYN so both
      change together — Google cross-checks a site against its profile.
- [ ] **Add all four varieties as Products,** each with its price per pallet.
      This is what puts prices into Google's own surfaces.
- [ ] **Twenty or more real photos.** See §5.
- [ ] **Seed the Q&A section.** Owners are allowed to post questions and answer
      them. Use the questions customers actually ask; the site's FAQ is a
      ready-made list.
- [ ] **Post weekly during the season** (roughly April to September). A Post
      takes two minutes: what was cut this week, what is ready, a finished lawn.

## 2. Google reviews — the real competitive gap

Reported review counts for Atlanta competitors: Super-Sod around 305, Atlanta Sod
Farms 169, Simply Sod 158, 404 SOD 75, NG Turf Douglas County 56. Those figures
come from the marketing review, not from independent checking, but the shape of
the gap is not in doubt.

Reviews are the hardest thing on this page to fake and the slowest to build,
which is exactly why they are worth the effort. Thirty to fifty genuine reviews
over twelve months would put Go Green Sod in the conversation.

- [ ] **Ask two to three weeks after delivery,** once the lawn has taken and the
      customer is pleased with how it looks. Asking on delivery day asks about a
      stack of pallets.
- [ ] **Use the short review link** from the Google Business Profile dashboard.
      Text it. Do not make anyone search.
- [ ] **Ask them to mention the grass and the city,** naturally: *"It helps a lot
      if you mention which grass you went with and the town it went to."* A
      review reading "Zeon Zoysia delivered to Alpharetta" carries local and
      product context no amount of on-page work can manufacture.
- [ ] **Reply to every review, including the bad ones.** A calm reply to a
      complaint reads better to the next customer than the complaint reads badly.
- [ ] **Never offer anything in exchange for a review** — not a discount, not a
      free pallet, not a prize draw. This breaks Google's policy and a 2026
      guideline specifically targets undisclosed incentivised reviews. Penalties
      land on the profile, which is the asset.
- [ ] **Never ask only the happy customers.** Selectively soliciting positive
      reviews ("review gating") is also a policy violation. Ask everyone.

> One thing NEXTLYN deliberately did **not** build: review stars in the site's
> own search listings. Google makes any page ineligible for star ratings when the
> business controls the reviews about itself, and faking it risks a manual
> penalty. Reviews on the Google profile are the asset. Marking them up on the
> site is not.

## 3. The other platforms

The point of all of these is **one identical name, address and phone number
everywhere.** Inconsistent details are what make Google uncertain a business is
real. Decide the exact format once — including whether it is "678.237.3111" or
"(678) 237-3111" — and never vary it.

Free and worth doing, roughly in order:

- [ ] **Apple Business Connect.** Feeds Apple Maps and Siri. Almost every small
      business ignores it, which is the reason to do it.
- [ ] **Bing Places.** Feeds Bing and Microsoft Copilot. Ten minutes, and it can
      often import straight from the Google profile.
- [ ] **Nextdoor Business.** Suburban Atlanta homeowners ask their neighbours
      where to buy sod. This is where that conversation happens.
- [ ] **The existing Facebook page** — check the name, address, phone, hours and
      website link all match. It is already linked from the site.
- [ ] **Yelp** and **Better Business Bureau.**

Worth more than any directory, and harder to get — real industry authority:

- [ ] **Georgia Urban Ag Council**, **Georgia Green Industry Association**,
      **Turfgrass Producers International.** Membership pages are genuine,
      relevant links from sites Google already trusts on this subject.
- [ ] **Local chambers of commerce** for the towns actually served.

**Do not buy a citation package or a "500 directory submissions" service.** Those
links are worthless at best, and cleaning up wrong addresses afterwards costs
more than the service did.

## 4. Facts only the client can answer

These are questions NEXTLYN will not invent answers to. The site is being built
to be quoted by ChatGPT and Google's AI answers, and **being quoted saying
something wrong about sod is worse than not being quoted at all** — it is the
farm's reputation on the line, not the website's.

Each answer unlocks page content that is already scaffolded and waiting.

- [ ] **Watering.** What should a customer do on day one, the first week, the
      second week? How much, how often?
- [ ] **How long can sod sit on the pallet** in a Georgia summer before it has to
      go down? This is the single most asked question in the trade and the one
      most often answered wrongly online.
- [ ] **Best time of year to lay sod in Atlanta,** and whether there is a window
      to avoid.
- [ ] **Mowing height** for each of the four grasses, and when the first cut
      should happen.
- [ ] **Starter fertiliser** — recommended or not? Which kind?
- [ ] **Delivery logistics.** Where does the truck put the pallets? Is driveway
      access needed? What happens if nobody is home? Can a specific drop spot be
      requested?
- [ ] **Contractor terms.** Is there contractor pricing? Net terms? Can a
      landscaper set up standing weekly deliveries? This unlocks a page aimed at
      repeat wholesale buyers, which is the best revenue on this list.
- [ ] **Two or three customers** willing to be quoted by name and town.

## 5. Photography

Extends the note in `LAUNCH.md`. The four turf close-ups on the site today are
open-license stock images. **Stock grass on a sod farm's own website is a
credibility problem and possibly a licensing one.** A phone camera in good
daylight beats stock comfortably.

Needed:

- [ ] Each of the four grasses, close enough to see the blade, labelled correctly
      so the site does not show Bermuda on the Zeon page.
- [ ] Harvesting in the field, and pallets being loaded.
- [ ] The truck, and pallets sitting on a real customer driveway.
- [ ] Finished lawns, ideally before and after.
- [ ] Descriptive filenames — `zeon-zoysia-pallet-alpharetta.jpg`, not
      `IMG_2394.jpg`.

He drags replacements into the same Studio documents. No code change, no deploy.

> **Geotagging photos does nothing.** Google strips EXIF data from uploads, and
> there is no evidence it ever helped ranking. Anyone selling geotagging as local
> SEO is selling nothing. Real, varied, correctly labelled photos are what help.

## 6. Keeping it going

The website stops improving the day nobody adds to it. This is the whole reason
it has a CMS.

- [ ] **One or two blog posts a month**, written in Studio. No developer needed.
      Answer real customer questions — the ones in §4 are the best starting list,
      and each one is a search someone is making right now.
- [ ] **Time posts to the season.** Sod demand in Atlanta runs roughly April to
      September. Publish the "when should I lay sod" post in March, not August.
- [ ] **Weekly Google Business Profile posts** during the season.
- [ ] **Keep asking for reviews.** Every delivery is an opportunity, and the
      compounding is the entire point.
